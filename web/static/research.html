Classifier Information Coefficient

Each day, the scored names are ranked by AI score and ranked again by the return they actually delivered; the correlation between those two orderings is that day's IC. Computing it within a day and only then averaging makes it market-neutral by construction — it only ever asks whether the higher-scored names beat the lower-scored ones that day.

IC (Pearson) correlates the raw score against the raw return, so a single large mover dominates it. Rank IC (Spearman) correlates only the two orderings, so it asks the question you actually care about — did the score sort them correctly — and is not hijacked by one outlier. Read Rank IC. On 2026-08-28 the two were +0.26 and +0.088 on the same data, the gap being one stock that fell 12.6%.

ICIR is the mean divided by its own spread across days: consistency, not size. A score that is right by a little every single day beats one that averages the same while swinging wildly.
